General
	•	Always bring the essentials: certificate, medical, and ID
	•	United: Super easy to list right in their app.
	•	American: Talk to the gate agent.
	•	Be upfront about your situation; there to observe. Gate agents can text the crew, let you board early, and sometimes the captain will even let you help set up the flight deck if they’re cool with it.

3/29/25: KIND → KORD (CA Leg)
	
	•	They actually boarded me first (I almost missed it because I was zoning out with headphones—bad move). The captain needs to brief jumpseaters early, so stay alert.
	•	The crew made sure “additional crew” was listed in the MCDU—neat to see it from the jumpseat side.
	•	The captain gave a thorough release brief, including standard items plus NOTAMed VORs plugged into the FMS. Asked if FO was familiar with the wx on the release.
	•	The crew chatted about weather, thunderstorm tops, turbulence—basically the usual suspects.
	•	Since it was an early flight for them and they’d never flown together before, they identified that as a “threat” in their TPC.
	•	T/O data came back lightning-fast via ACARS, right around “below the line" was done. The MCDU interface looked a bit different from what we’re used to on the flat panel.
	•	They pulled up a page on ACARS that showed their arrival gate—pretty handy.
	•	FO monitored the ramp until taxi (he basically said a quick “bye” right before they rolled).
	•	The takeoff callouts were super smooth—“TOGA set” sounded really coordinated, visually checked all indications before saying "set".
	•	Captain hand-flew until the FO announced “After Takeoff Checklist complete.” Great technique, very controlled.
	•	ATIS was requested pretty early during climb—short flight, so they didn’t waste time.
	•	The FO (PM) made a descent announcement.
	•	Standard ILS approach, with a speed plan of 210 to the fix prior to FAF and down to 170 at the FAF (the captain called that out as a threat—easy to get high/fast).
	•	Additional “float” threat with extended winglets, plus icing speeds to consider.
	•	Captain switched on all the lights crossing the runway (nice safety habit).
	•	FO turned on ACMP 1 & 2 before shutting down Engine 2.
	•	He also called ramp right after they cleared the runway.
	•	Turned on the APU once they were on the alley—didn’t need the captain’s prompt.

3/29/25: KORD → KIND (Captain’s Leg)

	•	Write ATC taxi instructions down somewhere safe (i.e., not in the MCDU scratchpad). Old-school pen and paper never fails.
	•	This aircraft (especially with extended winglets) loves to pick up speed. The captain and FO were on guard the whole time, using speed brakes a lot. They’d briefed “overspeed” as a threat and tackled it proactively.
	•	The captain suggested not slowing too much until you’re basically at the FAF—fits the flow of the approach well.
	•	If you have to change runways mid-arrival, the FMS might rebuild the entire STAR. If you hit NAV without verifying, the plane can snap right back onto the STAR. Apparently, that’s just one of many “fun” gotchas in EPIC 27.
	•	CA recommended hand-flying as much as you’re comfortable with, especially at lower-complexity airports—great for building skill.
	•	But if you’re flying with a low-time captain or expecting turbulence, autopilot can save a lot of mental bandwidth.
	•	The captain hand-flew from about 2,000 feet all the way down, using PAPI and basically treating it like a single-engine piston. He advises not to get overly reliant on the flight. Don't get in the habit of doing that from the get-go.
	•	CA called ground for help since ground has all the resources and knows the local drill—especially at Indy.
	•	The ACARS message about your arrival gate also shows estimated turn times. If it’s a really short turnaround (like 50 minutes), you can sometimes wait on your post-flight walk-around. The new crew will likely show up soon and can knock out the preflight inspection for you—particularly nice in bad weather.
	•	On both flights, the FO (as PM) had to make the descent announcement and the “FA, prepare for landing” call. Per SOP, those are two separate calls, although some people combine them in practice. He did it about 10-15 minutes prior to reaching TOD, so passengers had a heads-up before the seatbelt sign came on. The captain said to consult the Customer Service Guide and get comfortable with those PAs.
