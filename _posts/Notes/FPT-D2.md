# FLOWS WITH TRIGGERS

---

## ARRIVING AT THE AIRCRAFT

1. **Verify Required Documentation/Manuals Onboard**  
   - Both FO & CA ensure that all aircraft documents, manuals, and required certifications are present and valid.

2. **Review Aircraft Maintenance & Flight Log (AFML)**  
   - CA is responsible for reviewing the AFML.  
   - If there are any irregularities at the gate, follow FOM Chapter 29.8.1 to troubleshoot.  
   - Typically, the CA will notify Dispatch via ACARS or phone to connect with Maintenance for corrective actions.  
   - Once resolved, CA documents the corrective actions with Maintenance in the AFML. Then CA notifies Dispatch that a release amendment may be needed and follows their instructions to complete the necessary log.  
   - CA coordinates with FO to amend the release if needed.

3. **Re-run Originating/Receiving Flow & Checklist If Needed**  
   - If the Originating/Receiving Flow & Checklist was accomplished prior to addressing Maintenance irregularities, it must be performed again after the issues are resolved.

---

## SAFETY & POWER ON CHECKLIST

2. **Powering Up the Aircraft**  
   - Once the Captain confirms it’s safe to power up (i.e., no deferrals preventing power-up), the FO runs the Safety and Power On Checklist to power up the aircraft.

---

## ORIGINATION/RECEIVING FLOW & CHECKLIST

3. **MCDU Initialization**  
   1. FO verifies the current date and confirms both navigation databases are valid, then loads & verifies position.  
   2. FO enters the flight number and destination on the RTE page, ensuring the CA’s side is also set correctly.

4. **Complete the Origination/Receiving Flow**  
   - FO lets the CA know they are ready for the Origination/Receiving Checklist, and both complete it together.

---

## BEFORE START TO THE LINE FLOW & CHECKLIST

5. **Begin MCDU Flight Plan Entry**  
   1. Confirm and send in Fit for Duty. CA & FO input their PIN.

6. **Obtain ATIS**  
   1. FO obtains and notes the ATIS code, including wind, temperature, altimeter, runway(s) in use, and any other relevant weather or airport-specific information.

7. **Confirm PDC**  
   1. FO requests and verifies the departure clearance, confirms it matches the release, notes any discrepancies, sets heading and altitude bugs, and enters the assigned transponder code.

8. **Request Takeoff Data**  
   1. FO requests takeoff data.

9. **Build Flight Plan**  
   1. FO loads the route into the MCDU and ensures takeoff limits are entered.

10. **Continue Before Start Flow**  
   - FO verifies all necessary tasks in the flow are completed before calling for a Release Brief from the CA.

11. **Departure Review**  
   1. FO will begin by asking the PM (CA) for input on threats (weather, NOTAMs, MEL/CDL items).  
   2. FO uses FD Pro tabs to review company notes, taxi routes, and the departure procedure.

12. **APU and GPU Considerations**  
   1. If boarding is starting or the GPU needs to be disconnected, the CA may request starting the APU.  
   2. Once the APU is running, CA may coordinate with the ramp agent to disconnect the GPU.

13. **Before Start Checklist To the Line**  
   - FO lets the CA know they are ready for the checklist, and they complete it together.

   1. The Flight Attendant advises that the cabin is ready.  
   2. CA locks the flight deck door.  
   3. FO begins the flow starting with the beacon.

15. **Before Start Below the Line Flow**

16. **Before Start Checklist Below the Line**  
   - FO prompts the CA to complete the checklist together.

---

## PUSHBACK AND ENGINE START

17. **Pushback Coordination**  
   1. FO switches to the ramp frequency and coordinates push clearance.  
   2. CA coordinates with the pushback operator, possibly stating “Emergency Brake Off, standby for push.”  
   3. FO monitors communications for push clearance and notifies CA once received.

18. **Tug Disconnected and Hand Signals**  
   1. After the aircraft is positioned, CA and FO verify that the tug is disconnected.  
   2. Look for the ramp crew’s “tug clear” or “wave-off” signal (tow/tug/wave).

19. **Engine Start**  
   - FO starts engine(s) per CA request, monitoring EICAS for engine status.  
   - FO identifies any abnormality, takes actions, and advises CA.

20. **Takeoff Data Verification**  
   1. Once takeoff data is received, FO ensures it is correctly entered in PERF TAKEOFF and TRS.

---

## AFTER START FLOW & CHECKLIST

21. **After Start Flow**  
   - FO starts the After Start Flow and coordinates with CA to complete the After Start Checklist.

22. **Second Engine Start (If Using Single-Engine Taxi)**  
   1. When single-engine taxi is used, once takeoff is imminent, FO starts the second engine upon CA’s request and monitors EICAS.  
   2. Start a 2-minute timer for engine warm-up.  
   3. FO sets the electric hydraulic pumps 1 & 2 to AUTO after the second engine start.

---

## TAXI FLOW & CHECKLIST

23. **Request Taxi Clearance**

24. **Taxi Flow**  
   - Once both engines are powered on and taxi instructions are received, FO begins the Taxi Flow.

25. **Takeoff Briefing**  
   1. FO as the pilot flying gives the takeoff briefing.  
   2. Display the MCDU FPL page to brief departure runway, initial heading or FMS routing, first fix, assigned altitude (on the PFD), and any special procedures/threats.

26. **Taxi Checklist**  
   1. FO coordinates with CA to complete the Taxi Checklist.  
   2. FO sets MFD to MAP page.

## POINT/BEARING/DISTANCE

- Essentially creating a fix to put on the flight plan.  
- Build it out verbatim in the scratchpad, feed the amber, CAMI it with PM on FPL page and MAP profile, then activate it.

---

## HEADING TO INTERCEPT RADIAL

- Draws out a radial from a fix (whether it already exists in the routing or new fixes).  
- Prepare the fix in the scratchpad, feed the amber as if directing it, then 2L to activate it.  
- Under course, enter the inbound course (may require subtracting 180 from the instructed radial).  
- CAMI it before activating.

---

## RADIAL/RADIAL

- Again, verbatim in the scratchpad, feed the amber, then CAMI before activating.

## SKILLS & TIPS

- CA asks for route preview upon reaching cruise altitude prior to making a PA.  
- If an EICAS message is addressed in flight but remains present prior to landing, you may call out “EICAS Checked and Associated” during the landing checklist.  
- On Go-Arounds, use **3C**: **Checklist**, **Communicate**, **Cross-feed** (if single engine).  
- After setting the missed approach altitude, state “xxx set for the missed.”  
- State “I have the comms.” when PM is troubleshooting with QRH.  
- Below 10,000ft, use manual speed.  
- Descending through 1500ft or FAF, have one hand on the throttle and one on the control wheel.  
- Max reverse upon touchdown; release the throttle levers to reduce to min reverse when FO calls 80 kts, then return levers to idle to stow reversers at 60 kts.  
- FO calls ramp control to inform them that the plane has landed and is expecting a gate.
